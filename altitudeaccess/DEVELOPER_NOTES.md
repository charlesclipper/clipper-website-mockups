# DEVELOPER_NOTES — AltitudeAccess® page

Everything below is about the **source brochure** (AltitudeAccess® brochure PDF, printed pages 26–43; tri-fold used only as a cross-check). The mockup reproduces pricing and terms **exactly as printed**; where the brochure contradicts itself, the contradiction is reproduced, not fixed. Nothing here is legal advice; these are flags for Charles / counsel before the page goes live.

## A. How the brochure maps to the page (and what was ambiguous)

- The PDF has 23 PDF-pages made of two-page **spreads**. Mapping used: PDF p.14 = printed 26–27, p.15 = 28–29, p.16 = 30–31, p.17 = 32–33, p.18 = 34–35, p.19 = 36–37, p.20 = 38–39, p.21 = 40–41, p.22 = 42–43, p.23 = back cover (44, contact line + address). Verified by the printed folios in the text layer.
- **"The Case for AltitudeAccess" was requested as pgs 26–37.** The section of that name is only on **pp. 36–37**. Pp. 26–27 are "By the Numbers" (stats: #1 volume turboprop operator at Van Nuys; <2% pilot acceptance rate; 99.4% on-time dispatch (trailing 12 months); 100% PIC type-rated pilots; $50M liability coverage per flight; 2 pilots crewing every flight) and pp. 28–29 is a "Membership — Simple and transparent." divider. Because the brief says only p. 28+ matter, the pp. 26–27 stats were **not** used. If wanted, they would slot in as a stat row under the Case section; they are claims that need substantiation (see §D). Only "Simple and transparent." from p. 28–29 is used (hero tagline).
- **Hero/intro** has no dedicated brochure page; it uses the program's own wording from p. 43 ("a program built on capable aircraft, consistent service and a rate you can plan around"), the p. 28–29 tagline, and four facts from pp. 30 and 40. The p. 43 pull quote is used in full in How to Join.
- **Trip-time tables (p. 35)** have no column headers: values like "0h 30m" sit next to destination names under the title "Transparent pricing, wherever your destination." The mockup labels the column neutrally as **"Time"**. It presumably means estimated occupied flight time from Van Nuys (the footnote says pricing reflects trips with origin or destination at Van Nuys), but the brochure never says so, and the table shows no dollar prices. The footnote is marked with an asterisk that has no matching asterisk in the tables; it is reproduced as printed.
- **Peak days (p. 33)** exist only as a shaded calendar image. They were transcribed by eye from a 200-dpi render: **Jan 1–2; May 27–31; Jul 3–5; Sep 4–6; Nov 25–27; Dec 23–26 and 30–31** (2027 only). Please have the owner confirm the transcription against the InDesign source.
- **Tier 10 has no descriptive text** on p. 34 (25 and 50 have the "rate protection" note); the mockup leaves it blank, as printed.
- The **address** (7900 Balboa Blvd., Van Nuys, CA 91406) is from the back cover (p. 44) and the tri-fold; p. 43 itself lists email, phone and web only. The brochure's contact email for this program is **access@clipperaviation.com** (the site footer uses **info@**).

## B. Inconsistencies inside the brochure (reproduced as printed — do not "fix" without owner/legal decision)

1. **"No peak pricing", "No variable pricing", "No surprises", and (live Charter page) "no hidden fees"** vs. the **"Not included" list** and other charges: special-event surcharges, crew overnights ($2,500/night), weather hangaring and de-icing away from base, international handling fees, special-request catering, unoccupied repositioning flat fees ($1,000–$3,000), a $1,000 same-day round-trip surcharge, and pet travel at $500/flight for 10-hour members. "Special-event surcharges" in particular sits uneasily next to "No peak pricing" and "The same rate on a Tuesday in February as a Friday before Thanksgiving." The brochure's answer is "pass-throughs are disclosed and itemized before billing" — but p. 34 also says the rate is "all-in".
2. **"No blackout dates" vs. peak days.** The program says no blackout dates and names Super Bowl Sunday, Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year's Eve, Coachella and Formula 1 Las Vegas. Peak days are not blackouts, but they carry stricter cancellation terms (see 5). Also: the 2027 calendar shades Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's, but **no February, March, April, June, August or October dates at all** — i.e. the Super Bowl and Coachella are not in the shaded set — and November's shading is Nov 25–27 only. Is the F1 Las Vegas weekend a peak period or not? It is presented in the "no blackout" list but the calendar does not mark it.
3. **"Guaranteed availability" vs. "first-come-first-served".** p. 30: "Seventy-two hours' notice secures your aircraft. While membership bookings are handled on a first-come-first-served basis, our sizable fleet … means members are **highly unlikely** to ever face availability constraints." p. 40 repeats "ensures guaranteed aircraft availability", then "Trips inside 72 hours are confirmed when the schedule allows." Also "Limited membership … your aircraft is never double-booked or otherwise unavailable" (p. 31) and "Guaranteed recovery … a replacement is sourced" are absolute statements. The tri-fold also says "Seventy-two hours' notice guarantees availability."
4. **Repositioning table gap at exactly 800 miles (p. 35).** Bands: "under 200", "Between 200 and 399", "Between 400 and 599", "Between 600 and 799", "**Greater than 800**". A distance of exactly 800 miles (and fractional miles, e.g. 399.5) is not covered. Not stated: statute vs. nautical miles, whether the fee applies each direction, who triggers a repositioning leg, or whether occupied-hour billing also applies.
5. **Cancellation wording.** Non-peak: "Within 72 hours … 50% non-refundable"; "Within 48 hours … 100% non-refundable"; "Outside of 72 hours, no penalty" — works only if read as 48–72 h = 50%, <48 h = 100%, and the lines overlap as written. Peak: "Within 2 weeks … 50%"; "Within 72 hours … 100%". **Not stated:** what applies to peak days outside 2 weeks (presumably no penalty — not printed, so not shown); how a peak day is determined for multi-day trips (departure date only? any day of the trip?); what happens if **Clipper** cancels (weather, mechanical); how "% of the trip price" converts into hours debited from the prepaid balance; and the interaction with the "72 hours' notice" booking rule (a booking made inside 72 h is immediately inside the 50%/100% windows).
6. **Unused-hours expiry is not stated.** The contract "covers one year" and "full refunds without penalty are available on exit for hours not yet flown" (p. 42), while 25- and 50-hour tiers "lock in preferred rates for an additional 3/6 months beyond contract term (ie. 15/18-month rate protection)" (p. 34). It is unclear whether hours expire at 12 months, roll over, can be used during the extra months, or are refundable after the contract term. The tri-fold says "Rate held for the full one-year contract." for the 10-hour tier. Is the refund on exit subject to the cancellation charges above? Is there any admin/early-termination cost?
7. **"Inclusive of taxes and fees" (p. 34)** vs. the "Not included" list, which itself contains fees (international handling) and per-trip pass-throughs, while "FBO and landing fees" are included. Needs a tax/legal review of which taxes are actually included (e.g. federal excise tax on charter air transportation is not mentioned anywhere in the brochure) before "inclusive of taxes and fees" and "All-in" are published. The tri-fold prints the same phrase beside the $5,500 rate.
8. **"Same fixed hourly rate … no up-sell for committing further" (pp. 30, 34)** vs. tier-dependent benefits: rate protection (3/6 extra months) and free pet travel for 25- and 50-hour tiers only ($500/flight at 10 hours). Pet travel is also listed under "Included" while carrying a fee for one tier.
9. **"Crew overnights"** appears both as a charge ("$2,500 per night", p. 35) and under "Not included" (p. 41); consistent, but the brochure doesn't say per crew, per aircraft, or whether the pre-itemization rule applies.
10. **"Occupied flight hour"** is never defined (round trips, wait time, ground time); unoccupied legs are covered by flat fees only.
11. **Funds handling.** "Independent account", "client funds are held in independent accounts with full client visibility", "Clipper draws only as flights are completed — never in advance" (pp. 31, 41, 42) vs. "Hours are deducted from your account at flight completion" — what type of account (escrow/trust), which institution, and what regulatory treatment applies are not stated. Counsel should confirm the wording and whether any DOT/state requirements apply.
12. **Program naming.** The title requested for SEO uses "**Jet Card**", but the fleet is a turboprop (King Air 350) and the brochure itself says "the jet category costs more and often delivers less". The live Charter page also calls it "A Jet Card program". The brochure calls it a "preferred rate program" and says "card" once (p. 31, "your card serves whoever you plan to fly with"). Also, the live site spells it both "AltitudeAccess®" and "Altitude Access"/"AltitudeAccess" without ®; standardise. Tri-fold includes the legal line "AltitudeAccess® is a preferred rate program of Clipper Aviation. Full terms are set out in the program agreement." — **that disclaimer is absent from brochure pp. 28–43**; recommend adding an equivalent line to the page (not added, to avoid inventing copy).

## C. Third-party names and marks used in the copy (legal/brand review)

- **"Super Bowl"** (NFL), **"Coachella"**, **"Formula 1 Las Vegas"** (Formula One) — named as example dates (pp. 30, 37). Trademark owners are known to police advertising use; consider "major sporting events and festivals" if counsel advises.
- **"Charter Flight Support"** — named as the AOG trip-recovery insurer (p. 31).
- **"King Air 350"** (Textron Aviation / Beechcraft), **"FBO"** terms, **Van Nuys Airport** — fine editorially; check the brand/trade-name policy for Textron references.
- Photos: owners/licences unknown (some look like stock). One brochure photo shows an aircraft registration; the hero image shows part of a tail number ("N…Y"). Check registrations are OK to publish.

## D. Comparative / superlative claims needing substantiation

- p. 36: "The King Air 350 is the **largest cabin-class turboprop in regular charter service**"; "carries more passengers in greater comfort than comparable platforms, all at a **materially lower hourly rate**"; "the jet category costs more and often delivers less."
- p. 37: "Fixed hourly pricing is **unusual in this segment**"; "**Most** programs publish headline rates that dissolve the moment weather shifts…"; "**Most** programs source aircraft from a patchwork of managed operators…"
- p. 30: "The days other programs publish as off-limits are the days members fly most with us." p. 41: "Other programs publish a headline rate, then itemize the rest."
- p. 38: "Maintaining a dedicated crew **usually costs owners in excess of $300K annually**"; "transactions seven-figure sums in either direction"; p. 39: "Fractional ownership … at **comparable or greater cost**."; "a **tiny** population of flyers."
- p. 38: "Clipper holding a **sizable** footprint of aircraft maintenance and storage facilities at Van Nuys"; p. 30/36: "our **sizable fleet**"; "membership never outpacing fleet capacity" (p. 37) vs. "Enrollment is capped" (p. 31) — what is the cap?
- Operational promises: itinerary "returned within the hour"; departure "typically occurs within minutes"; "dedicated trip concierge".
- pp. 26–27 stats (not used): #1 operator, <2% acceptance, 99.4% on-time, 100% type-rated, $50M liability, 2 pilots.
